Research

(the short version)

I'm a PhD candidate in Human Genetics at McGill, in Dr. Shafaat Rabbani's lab at the Research Institute of the MUHC. I study breast cancer from both ends: cells, blots, and mice on one side, R, Python, and a cluster on the other.

I'm doing a PhD at McGill in Montreal, studying breast cancer. Half my time I'm growing cancer cells and running experiments on them. The other half I'm on a computer, making sense of what they told me.

now

uPAR in breast cancer

What the uPAR receptor drives in invasion, stemness, and spread to bone, and what happens when you take it away.

Cancer cells have little antennas on their surface. One of them, called uPAR, helps them break loose and travel around the body, even into bone. I study what happens when you take that antenna away.

now

Treatment resistance

How triple-negative and HER2-positive breast tumors adapt and keep growing under therapy.

Sometimes a treatment works for a while, and then the cancer learns to ignore it. I try to figure out how it learns that, so we can stop it from happening.

now

Drug combinations

Pairing a genetic hit with a drug so tumors lose their escape routes, then scoring the synergy.

If a tumor has two escape doors, one drug only shuts one of them. I test pairs of treatments that shut both doors at the same time.

with friends

Clinical epigenetics

DNA methylation markers with clinical questions behind them: a cell-free DNA panel that tracks prostate cancer grade from a blood draw, and methylation age in a lung health cohort.

Our DNA carries tiny chemical sticky notes. With friends at McGill, I read them from a simple blood draw, to help spot aggressive prostate cancer and to learn how the body ages.

Primer efficiency

At 100% efficiency this is the standard 2−ΔΔCt method. Other efficiencies weight each Ct by log2(E), which gives the Pfaffl ratio. Technical replicates are averaged first; biological replicates set n and SD. No statistical test is run.

Tumor volume

Caliper measurements to volume, one tumor over time. Length is the longest diameter; width is perpendicular to it.

On westerns

Load control first, argue later.

If the loading control is ugly, the blot is fine the blot is not fine.

Crop after you save the full membrane. Always.
